Darn Good Yarn / PROPOSAL FOR REVIEW

From product discovery to a better-prepared enquiry.

Calculate yarn needed for a group of projects from the maker’s own yardage estimate and skein length; round up whole skeins.

Prepared by Samuel Acuña · November 3, 2026 · Free demonstrations

What we observed

  • The selected yarn listings are DK-weight recycled silk products.
  • The official store offers knitting and crochet kits as well as yarn.
  • Yarn quantities depend on the pattern and gauge; this planner uses values supplied by the maker.

Hypothesis to validate

  • A product edit connected to an operational brief may make enquiries easier to qualify. This is a hypothesis to measure, not a diagnosis of the current operation.
  • Calculate yarn needed for a group of projects from the maker’s own yardage estimate and skein length; round up whole skeins.
  • Budget, volume, priorities and existing systems must be confirmed with the team.

Three assets, one journey

  • Website: a visual edit of three real references, an editable local shortlist and official product links.
  • Automation: Yarn project requirements. Calculation, validation, export and follow-up state controls.
  • Bot: eight documented answers and a brief-building flow. The demo uses local rules; connected AI would follow authorization of sources and boundaries.

Suggested architecture

  • Authorized catalog → reference and format validation → CRM record → human review → recorded next action.
  • The official source retains prices and stock; the workflow does not create a second commercial truth.
  • One request identifier, duplicate-event controls and follow-up cancellation on reply or closure.
  • Proposed AI: retrieval from approved catalog/FAQs, source-backed responses and escalation when evidence is missing.

Implementation stages

  • 1. Validate the use case: current process, data source, owner and useful outcome.
  • 2. Bounded pilot: connect an authorized catalog and review queue without creating real orders.
  • 3. Tests: incomplete inputs, duplicates, reference changes, reply and closure.
  • 4. Supervised launch: least-privilege access, action logs and a manual fallback.

Acceptance criteria

  • The brief preserves the product, units, quantities and user-entered assumptions.
  • Invalid values are rejected; whole-pack rounding never orders a fraction of a pack.
  • Recording a reply or closure stops the next follow-up.
  • The bot identifies unverified information and hands off to the official channel; it does not invent availability or delivery.
  • Final visual review and integrated bot testing are required before treating the package as published.

Required access and data

  • Current catalog with references, variants, images and approved policies.
  • Minimum access to the CRM or request destination chosen by the team.
  • Actual inventory source, pricing rules, formats and an owner for exceptions.
  • Define consent, notification channel and retention policy before using customer data.

Boundaries and next step

  • No private accounts were accessed and no integration was connected. No requests were sent and no products were charged.
  • Photos and names identify official products. This independent proposal does not represent the company.
  • Choose one priority workflow, review this scenario and agree scope, access and how success will be measured.
